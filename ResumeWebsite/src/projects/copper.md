## R&D

During my time at Copper, I've worked on an enormous variety of different projects. My job amounts to assisisting my boss in the development of new product and proof-of-concept prototypes. Think the 0-10 unit range. This means I do a bit of *everything*. 

A typical project might involve turning a very rough concept in to a functioning piece of hardware and then iterating on that hardware as we learn things about the project through testing. As a contributor to such an effort, I might be trying to hire a job shop to produce hardware, based on a second or third draft of an idea that may only exist in CAD software. I might be prototyping a sheet metal part myself while I wait for the outsourced components to arrive. Or I could be producing some artifact using data collected from a nest of thermocouples or onboard sensors. 

Ambiguity is a non-negotiable element of my job, and I've really focused on improving my ability to make informed guesses about what might be, rather than idling and waiting for clarification. I have an oppurtunity to both continue developing my strongest fundamentals—be it drafting or handling large digital assemblies—and also to experiment with processes that I have little to no exposure to.

Here's a snapshot of what I've had the oppurtunity to during my time at Copper:
- Design my first injection molded part.
- Create tools for a real production line.
- Own the mechanical design of a PoC battery integrated appliance.
    - And even better, *more than once!*
- Make beautiful, tactile objects, like softwood knobs for Copper's flagship induction range.

- Train my coworkers on manufacturing processes that I developed for in-house prototyping.
